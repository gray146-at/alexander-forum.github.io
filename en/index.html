The Alexander Forum studies how societies and people come through crises.

An independent, not-for-profit think & do tank based in Vienna, founded in 2025 by Dorian Rammer. The Forum publishes working papers and dossiers on resilience, polycrisis, mental health and the digital public sphere: open access, with sources, and open for review.

AF Working Paper 2026/1 Draft 1.9 · June 2026

Psychische Gesundheit als öffentliche Infrastruktur

Mental health as public infrastructure. Concept and editing: Dorian Rammer. Working draft in German, not to be cited as a final study.

„Der Status quo ist nicht billig. Er ist nur schlecht verbucht.“

The status quo is not cheap; it is merely badly accounted for. The paper proposes treating mental health care in Austria as steerable public infrastructure: with clinical assessment, care as a benefit in kind where there is a clinical need, psychotherapy, clinical-psychological treatment, aftercare, reintegration and prevention. Its figures are model corridors that still need checking.

Why reflection and resilience belong together

We see resilience as a shared task across politics, science, culture and society. Societal and personal crises are intertwined: those under pressure can contribute less, and unresolved tensions weaken the whole.

Crises do not arise only from external events. They also arise from patterns of interpretation, blind spots, institutional routines and concepts that no longer hold. For us, reflection therefore does not mean withdrawal but clarification: examining connections, seeing side effects, tolerating ambivalence, sharpening language and making assumptions visible.

This clarification matters all the more in a climate of constant acceleration. Digital overload, algorithmic feeds, short-video formats, outrage loops and fast reward systems are changing how information is sought, absorbed and judged. Attention becomes a scarce resource; orientation becomes a task for society.

Where people, institutions and public debate are already under pressure, platform logics can deepen fractures instead of bridging them. Polarisation, numbness, crisis fatigue and the loss of a shared sense of reality are therefore not only individual problems but questions of democratic and social resilience.

Resilience begins where reflection becomes practical. It does not mean mere adaptation to overload, but the ability to recognise strain earlier, mobilise resources and change structures so that people, organisations and democratic culture remain able to act.

How we work

  • Interdisciplinary, close to the sources and with a clear commitment to language.

  • For us, reflection is not a safe distance but a precondition for acting responsibly.
